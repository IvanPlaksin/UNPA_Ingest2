INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY DIRECTIVE
FOR THE UNITED NATIONS SECRETARIAT

7 March 2013

Pursuant to ST/SGB/2007/6 (Organization of the Secretariat of the United Nations)
and in accordance with Article 97 of the Charter of the United Nations,
the following provisions are promulgated.


Section 1
Objective

1.1 The objective of this Information Security Policy Directive is to establish the
framework within which all information held by the United Nations Secretariat
is protected against unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification,
or destruction.

1.2 This directive shall apply to all staff members of the United Nations Secretariat
and to all contractors, consultants and other individuals who have been granted
access to United Nations information assets.


Section 2
Strategic Principles

2.1 Information security shall be managed on a risk basis, with controls selected
and implemented in proportion to the assessed risks.

2.2 The information security programme for the United Nations Secretariat
is managed utilizing a risk management approach that seeks to achieve an
appropriate balance between security and operational requirements.

2.3 All staff members shall be responsible for protecting United Nations information
assets in their custody and must exercise the care of a prudent person.


Section 3
Commitments

3.1 The United Nations Secretariat shall ensure that appropriate levels of security
are implemented to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of its
information assets.

3.2 The Secretariat shall develop and maintain an information security programme
that is aligned with internationally recognized standards, in particular
the ISO/IEC 27000 series.

3.3 Senior managers shall provide leadership and visible support for information
security and must ensure that information security requirements are integrated
into the planning of their programmes and operations.


Section 4
Organization of Information Security

4.1 The Chief Information Technology Officer is responsible for the management
of the information security programme of the United Nations Secretariat.

4.2 The Information Security Section shall support departments and offices in
the implementation of this directive and related information security standards.

4.3 Each department and office shall designate an Information Security Focal Point
who is responsible for coordinating information security activities within
their department or office.


Section 5
Roles and Responsibilities

5.1 The Chief Information Technology Officer shall:
(a) Develop and maintain the information security programme;
(b) Report annually to the Secretary-General on the state of information security;
(c) Issue and maintain information security standards and guidelines;
(d) Provide oversight and guidance on information security matters.

5.2 The Information Security Section is responsible for:
(a) Conducting risk assessments;
(b) Monitoring compliance with information security requirements;
(c) Providing security awareness training.

5.3 Information Security Focal Points shall:
(a) Promote security awareness within their department or office;
(b) Report security incidents to the Information Security Section;
(c) Assist in the implementation of information security standards.

5.4 All staff members must:
(a) Comply with this directive and related security standards;
(b) Protect the information assets under their custody;
(c) Report security incidents and vulnerabilities without delay.

5.5 The Secretary-General is responsible for the overall information security
posture of the United Nations Secretariat.


Section 6
Final Provisions

6.1 This directive replaces ST/AI/2002/5 (Protection of personal information
regarding clients of the programme for victims of abuse).

6.2 This directive relates to ST/SGB/2007/6 and supplements the provisions
contained therein regarding the management of information technology resources.

6.3 This directive shall enter into force on 7 March 2013.

6.4 The Secretary-General may issue administrative instructions implementing
this directive pursuant to the authority conferred by Article 97 of the Charter.


Annex A
Information Security Levels

A.1 RESTRICTED — Information requiring basic safeguards to prevent unauthorized
disclosure.

A.2 CONFIDENTIAL — Information whose unauthorized disclosure could cause
harm to the interests of the Organization.

A.3 STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL — Information whose unauthorized disclosure could
cause serious harm, including threats to life or security.
